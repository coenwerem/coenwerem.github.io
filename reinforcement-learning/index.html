Learning Baseline Reproduction · MuJoCo / Adroit

From Demonstrations to Learned Dexterous Relocation

I ported the authors’ Demonstration-Augmented Policy Gradient (DAPG) [1] implementation to a maintained Adroit simulation environment, trained a relocation policy from 25 demonstrations, and evaluated the initial and trained policies on held-out reset seeds. Behavior cloning initialized the policy; subsequent DAPG training produced successful object relocation.

One independent training run, 100 final evaluation episodes per mode. This is a reproduction and simulator port of an established method; DAPG is credited to Rajeswaran et al. The measured improvement is from BC initialization to the trained DAPG checkpoint.

Implementation and Evaluation

I preserved the original observation ordering, action clipping, episode horizon, and sustained-success criterion in the simulator adapter. The training and evaluation pipeline separates development seeds from final test seeds and evaluates deterministic mean actions and stochastic policy execution independently.

Prior-Data RL Adaptation · Drake Simulation

Integrating Residual RL with Bimanual Assembly Control

I adapted Reinforcement Learning with Prior Data (RLPD) [2] to an A7 bimanual arm–hand system, combining recorded native-controller transitions with online residual-policy interaction. A bounded joint-reference residual acts through the existing feedback controller while the robot stabilizes a housing, acquires and transfers a part, inserts it, and withdraws its hands.

The updated policy retained assembly competence after learning. The initial policy, native controller, and zero-residual baseline also completed their evaluations, so the experiment does not establish improved task completion or a benefit from prior-data replay.

Control and Learning Integration

I implemented the residual-policy interface, admitted prior transitions from native-controller execution, and preserved command history and finite-task terminal semantics. The fixed training run completed 100 actor updates and 2,000 critic updates. Recorded requested and executed commands verify that the updated policy exerted nonzero residual authority.

Published Research · IEEE CDC 2025

Risk-Sensitive Distributional Reinforcement Learning

For reach–avoid navigation, I developed quantile-based action-value iteration with a Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) penalty estimated from the distribution of constraint-violation costs [3]. I integrated the risk-sensitive loss with quantile value estimation and evaluated the learned navigation policies in MuJoCo / Safety-Gymnasium. The paper establishes a contraction result under its stated assumptions.

Algorithm and Implementation

The learner represents action-value distributions with quantile regression. Kernel density estimation provides a distribution of constraint-violation costs, from which CVaR measures adverse-tail risk. A risk penalty augments the quantile loss before the action-value network update.

Supporting Study · Isaac Lab / PhysX

G1 PPO Reward Evaluation

I trained policies in 1,024 parallel Isaac Lab environments to investigate geometric pregrasp rewards. The study demonstrates training and evaluation infrastructure; sustained positioning and grasp-and-lift remain unresolved.

View the Reward Comparison, Rollouts, and Evaluation Protocol

I trained Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) policies in 1,024 parallel environments for a fixed-base Unitree G1 equipped with a RealHand L6. The Grasp Distance Field (GDF) reward measures proximity to a bank of candidate arm–hand configurations, incorporating hand posture alongside arm motion. The comparison uses a palm-to-object distance reward with a gated lift term.

An arrival occurs when the pregrasp field value falls below 0.25 during an episode. The field is a smooth minimum of weighted joint-space distances, so the threshold is a configuration-space criterion, not a Cartesian distance in meters.

New Recording Evaluation (October 14, 2026). With evaluation seed 42, the GDF checkpoint reached the pregrasp set in 63/64 episodes; the palm-distance checkpoint reached it in 0/64. These fresh counts are distinct from the archived evaluation above.

The arrival metric measures entry into the pregrasp set. In the displayed GDF rollout, the policy enters the set briefly and later settles outside it; arrival does not establish sustained positioning. Neither policy achieved grasp-and-lift completion in the reported evaluation.

The full GDF and palm-distance configurations differ in the approach, closure, and field-decrease reward terms. This comparison evaluates the reward configurations as a whole; it does not isolate the effect of a single term.
