ParcelStow

Does Imitation Learning Preserve Robustness
Under Execution-Timing Variation?

Clinton Enwerem, John S. Baras, and Calin Belta

University of Maryland, College Park

G1-L6 Comparisons A7-L6 Parcel Insertion Bimanual Assembly Constrained Object Relocation Paper Code Data & Checkpoints Results

Matching expert task success at one operating condition does not establish robustness preservation as task conditions vary. ParcelStow evaluates the expert–learner performance difference across a controlled variation in execution timing. The difference can widen, narrow, or reverse sign.

A scalar speedup factor r divides selected phase durations: at r = 2, those phases take half as long. Acquisition and settling durations are fixed. We evaluate expert and learner policies across the same speedup factors on three simulated manipulation tasks.

ParcelStow with the G1-L6 Humanoid

The G1-L6 humanoid executes parcel insertion, upright placement, and keyed peg insertion using an expert controller and an Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) policy. Each task requires the robot to acquire an object and satisfy a distinct placement or insertion condition. The paired videos compare expert and learner behavior at a task speedup factor of r = 2; playback is also accelerated to 2×. The comparison distinguishes task execution under shortened phase durations from video playback speed.

Expert–ACT Comparisons

In the recorded evaluations, ACT matches or exceeds expert nominal task success on all three tasks. At the higher speedup factors shown below, the expert succeeds more often. For parcel insertion, this difference appears within the demonstrated speed range; for upright placement and keyed peg insertion, it appears beyond that range.

Expert-minus-ACT differences at the highlighted factors are 31, 40.5, and 73.5 percentage points, with pointwise 95% paired bootstrap intervals [18, 44], [32, 49], and [67.5, 79.5]. Each task reports one trained ACT policy; training replications are reported individually in the records.

These evaluations use fixed state-based policies in Isaac Lab simulation. The release also contains Diffusion Policy and DAgger results, together with their pairing status and comparison limits. Explore the evaluation records.

Parcel Insertion with the A7-L6 Manipulator

The A7-L6 bimanual robot manipulator executes dexterous parcel insertion in Isaac Lab. We compare an expert controller with an ACT policy from the same recorded initial parcel pose at the nominal task rate, r = 1. The expert completes the task, whereas ACT fails the final-orientation criterion. This matched trial makes the expert–learner difference visible at the placement stage, where reaching the receptacle must also produce the required object orientation.

Bimanual Assembly through Imitation Learning

The A7-L6 bimanual robot manipulator coordinates housing stabilization with part acquisition, alignment, and insertion in Isaac Lab. The comparison pairs an expert controller with ACT and feedback control from identical recorded initial configurations. Both complete the nominal assembly: the expert in 54.74 s and ACT in 58.80 s. ACT exhibits brief right-arm jerks at the transfer-to-alignment and alignment-to-insertion transitions, while the expert transitions smoothly in this recording. These phase-boundary behaviors distinguish the executions even though both satisfy the task-completion criterion.

Constrained Object Relocation through Imitation Learning

The A7-L6 bimanual robot manipulator coordinates opening a passive flap on the target box with grasping, lifting, and placing an object inside the box. The learned policy maintains flap clearance during object transfer, then releases the object and withdraws the robot’s hands after placement. Trained by behavior cloning with corrective imitation data, the policy commands the arms and hands from simulated state observations and elapsed time. The video presents a successful Isaac Lab execution at the original task speed, emphasizing coordinated contact and object placement throughout the sequence.

Use the Benchmark

The benchmark includes 937 successful expert demonstrations and 970,565 control steps across the three tasks. Learned policies use 147-dimensional state observations and share a 16-dimensional joint-position action interface with the scripted experts at 50 Hz.

The frozen evaluation bundle contains 136 conditions from 20 source series and 23,200 episodes. Reproduce success counts and paired intervals on a CPU without running the simulator:
