dexreach is an Apache-2.0 Python package that plans dexterous grasps and manipulation for an arm and a multifingered hand treated as one robot. It is GPU-accelerated with NVIDIA cuRobo. Each grasp is a full arm-and-hand configuration that reaches the object without collision, and the planner reports whether the grasp can supply the wrenches its task demands within friction and actuator limits.

From each grasp, dexreach plans the whole-body approach, insertion, closing, lift, carry, place, release, and retreat motions. It also plans the contact forces that hold the object along these motions, and it plans two arms that move two objects at the same time. An Isaac Lab validator executes the exported plans with joint torques capped at the actuator limits.

Method

Whole-Body Grasp Synthesis

Grasp proposals are built from points sampled on the object surface. cuRobo's batched inverse kinematics places the hand at each proposal with the fingers open, avoiding the workcell and the object; this gives the pregrasp. From each pregrasp, a joint hand-arm optimization updates all arm and hand joints at once. Its objective combines the fingertip-to-surface gap, the min-weight grasp quality of FRoGGeR, penetration of the object, table, and robot itself, a posture term, joint-limit margins, and a patch term that keeps each fingertip contact one pad radius from an object edge. A pad on an edge rocks, and the object turns out of the grip.

Each resulting grasp is then checked for the wrenches its task requires. A linear program over the contact friction pyramids, bounded by the hand's joint torques or fingertip force limits, decides whether the grasp supplies the task wrench and ranks the feasible grasps by the scale of task wrench they can sustain.

Motions Planned from the Grasp

Motions are built backward from the optimized grasp, so the arm executes the configuration that was scored. Lift and retreat integrate damped least squares from the exact grasp, and the approach uses a straight joint-space path when it is collision-free and cuRobo's motion planner otherwise. Every motion is checked against the workcell and against objects resting on the table.

Force-Aware Contact Planning with Object Dynamics

For each grasp, one optimization plans the object state together with the fingertip and table contact forces, which are its inputs. The fingertips touch down at the end of the close, the squeeze rises to each grasp's balanced-hold margin, and the table supports the object during the hold and again after set-down. The executor applies the joint torques that produce the planned contact forces and ramps them down at release, so the release does not push the object. The forces can be planned against the bounds of an uncertainty set; the results below use the low end of the friction range and the high end of the object mass range.

Simultaneous Two-Arm, Two-Object Planning

Two arms that move two objects are planned as one robot. The left and right plans merge into one timeline in which every phase lasts as long as the slower arm needs, so both arms approach, close, lift, carry, place, release, and retreat together. The force-aware optimization covers both objects in one solve and constrains the clearance between the palm and five distal links of each hand, and between the two objects. When a minimum hand clearance of 20 cm is requested, the hands end 19.76 cm apart.

Embodiments

An embodiment is one registry file that names the arm, the hand's fingers and pad points, its joint couplings, and its open and closed preshapes.

Planned grasps and whole-body motions in the dexreach viewer.

Validation in Isaac Lab

The validator runs physics at 240 Hz and control at 60 Hz, caps each joint's torque at its actuator rating, and couples mimic joints as PhysX constraints. A rollout counts as retreated when the object is lifted, carried, placed within its goal tolerance, released, and left settled while the robot retreats. During the carry, the validator also monitors contact loss and stops the task when the object drops.

Grasp Selection across Embodiments and Objects

For each embodiment and object, ten grasps chosen by an in-place physics check each execute the full sequence three times, with the object's start pose, friction, and mass randomized (perturbation seed 2000).

Force-Aware Execution

With forces from the force-aware contact plan and the plan shifted to the measured object start pose, Franka-LEAP carries the soup can through the full sequence in 76/90 rollouts (26, 26, and 24 of 30 at perturbation seeds 2000, 2001, and 2002).

Two Arms, Two Objects

The dual A7-L6 robot carries a cylinder with its left hand and a soup can with its right, both arms moving at once under one force-aware plan. Nine grasp pairs each run three times per seed. Each rollout draws object start offsets of up to 5 mm and ±10°, a friction coefficient between 0.5 and 0.9, and a soup-can mass between 0.8 and 1.2 times nominal. The forces are planned for friction 0.5 and 1.2 times the soup-can mass, and the plan is shifted to the measured object start poses.
