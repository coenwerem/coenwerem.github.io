Manipulation in Human-Shared Workspaces

A robot working near a person must account for human motion while preserving the physical conditions needed to finish its task. During assembly, an avoidance motion can disrupt the grasp, displace the housing, or misalign a partially inserted part. The robot’s available motions therefore depend on its current contact with the objects and the stage of assembly.

We study this problem through bimanual assembly in a human-shared workcell. The objective is to accommodate nearby human motion while retaining the grasp, supporting the housing, and maintaining part–housing alignment through insertion and release. The demonstration emphasizes the interval before release, when the robot cannot simply withdraw its manipulating hand without interrupting the task.

Control Overview

The control problem couples two requirements: responding to observed human motion and maintaining the object contacts required by the current assembly stage. The diagram summarizes these inputs and the robot’s feedback interaction with the workspace.

Bimanual Assembly in a Shared Workspace

The A7-L6 bimanual robot manipulator acquires and transports a part, aligns it with a supported housing, and completes insertion while a simulated person reaches into the workcell. The robot then releases the part and withdraws. The two synchronized views connect the person’s movement through the workspace with the robot’s contact and alignment at the assembly.

Task Requirements Across the Assembly Sequence

Acquisition and Transport

The robot establishes a grasp and transports the part toward the housing. Grasp retention and object support must be maintained as the person moves near the transfer path.

Alignment and Insertion

The robot coordinates part motion with housing support. Their relative pose determines whether insertion can proceed, so changes in robot posture must remain compatible with the required alignment and contact.

Release and Withdrawal

After insertion, the robot releases the part before retracting its manipulating hand. This stage distinguishes motion constrained by assembly contact from withdrawal after that contact has ended.

From Grasp Execution to Assembly

Grasp Distance Fields addresses reactive grasp execution with collision avoidance and grasp-quality preservation. This research studies a distinct problem: accommodating human motion during a manipulation task whose contact requirements change through transport, insertion, and release. The connection is the need to preserve useful object contact while adapting the robot’s motion to its surroundings.
