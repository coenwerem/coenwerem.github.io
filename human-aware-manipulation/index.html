Manipulation in Human-Shared Workspaces

Institute for Systems Research, University of Maryland, College Park

Bimanual Assembly in a Shared Workspace

Manipulation in shared workspaces requires the robot to accommodate nearby human motion while maintaining the contacts needed to complete its task. We study bimanual assembly with the A7-L6 bimanual robot manipulator, including part transport, alignment, insertion, and withdrawal after release. The demonstration focuses on completing assembly while a person reaches into the workcell. Maintaining housing support and part–housing alignment is necessary while the robot remains in contact with the assembly; withdrawal follows release. The workspace view establishes the human–robot interaction, while the close view makes these object contacts visible.
