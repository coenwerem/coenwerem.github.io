We developed dexreach, an Apache-2.0 Python package for grasp and manipulation planning with a unified arm-hand configuration. The planner evaluates contact wrenches under friction and actuation limits, constructs whole-body motions from the evaluated grasp, and jointly plans object trajectories with fingertip and table-support forces. We evaluated complete manipulation sequences in Isaac Lab across arm-hand embodiments, object-waypoint tasks, and simultaneous dual-object manipulation.

Grasp and Motion Planning

Arm-Hand Configuration

Let \(q_{\mathrm{arm}}\) and \(q_{\mathrm{hand}}\) denote the arm and hand joint coordinates, with \(n_{\mathrm{arm}}\) and \(n_{\mathrm{hand}}\) joint coordinates, respectively. Their configuration domains are \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{arm}}\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_{\mathrm{arm}}}\) and \(\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{hand}}\subseteq\mathbb R^{n_{\mathrm{hand}}}\). The combined configuration is

\[ q=(q_{\mathrm{arm}},q_{\mathrm{hand}}) \in\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{arm}}\times\mathcal Q_{\mathrm{hand}}. \]

In the release pipeline, we evaluate hand grasp candidates, solve arm inverse kinematics, and reevaluate contacts in the reached arm-hand configuration. An alternative optimizer updates the arm and hand coordinates jointly. Joint limits, contact conditions, and geometric collision checks constrain the configurations accepted for motion planning.

Contact and Task-Wrench Feasibility

For a grasp with \(k\) usable contacts and specified actuation limits, \(f\in\mathbb R^{3k}\) stacks the forces on the object, expressed in object coordinates. At contact \(i\), the set \(\mathcal F_i(\mu)\) requires a nonnegative inward normal force, a tangential force magnitude no larger than the friction coefficient \(\mu\) times that normal force, and any specified fingertip force cap. The grasp map \(G(q)\in\mathbb R^{6\times3k}\) maps these forces to a wrench \(w\in\mathbb R^6\), comprising force in newtons and moment in newton-meters about the object's center of mass, expressed in object coordinates. Let \(\tau(q,f)\in\mathbb R^{n_\tau}\) be the modeled joint-torque vector and \(\tau_{\max}\in\mathbb R^{n_\tau}\) its componentwise limits, both in newton-meters, where \(n_\tau\) is the number of torque-limited model coordinates. A prescribed task-wrench set \(\mathcal W_T\) is feasible in this static contact model when it lies in the admissible wrench set:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal W_{\mathrm{adm}}(q;\mu) &=\left\{G(q)f:\ f_i\in\mathcal F_i(\mu)\ \text{for every }i, \quad |\tau(q,f)|\leq\tau_{\max}\right\},\\ \mathcal W_T&\subseteq\mathcal W_{\mathrm{adm}}(q;\mu). \end{aligned} \]

Whole-Body Motion and Contact Forces

The evaluated grasp configuration anchors the approach, insertion, close, lift, carry, place, release, and retreat motions. During held-object waypoint motion, the hand follows the prescribed object pose with a fixed object-relative hand transform and fixed finger joints. We check sampled robot configurations against the floor, table, robot geometry, and relevant objects, with object-collision checks determined by the motion phase. Unsuccessful motion candidates are rejected.

Force-aware planning couples the robot joint configuration with each object's pose and twist. Joint velocities and fingertip and table-support forces are the trajectory inputs. Discretized Newton–Euler equations relate these forces to object translation and rotation. The plan imposes scheduled open and closed contacts, friction constraints, and joint position, velocity, and torque bounds at trajectory knots. The default robot torque model is quasi-static; full robot dynamics are optional.

The executor tracks planned joint positions and velocities using position and velocity feedback with contact-torque feedforward. The feedforward contribution decreases during release. The reported force-aware experiments used a fixed execution window initialized from the measured object pose; the initial perturbation was known exactly from the simulator.

Simulation Evaluation

We ran Isaac Lab at 240 Hz for physics and 60 Hz for control. Joint torques were capped by rated effort where specified; otherwise, limits were derived from fingertip force caps and the planned contact Jacobian. Coupled fingers used mimic constraints, with no independent drive at follower joints. Robot-link gravity and articulation self-collision were disabled in these simulations; the planner evaluated geometric collision checks separately.

A complete manipulation sequence requires lift, carry, placement within the goal tolerances, release, object settling, cleared robot contact, and robot retreat. The goal-position tolerance is 0.02 m, and final settling drift is at most 0.005 m. Final pose checks assess tilt and, when prescribed, goal orientation. Both-object completion requires each object to complete its sequence.

Grasp Selection across Embodiments and Objects

For each embodiment-object case, we evaluated ten grasps selected by an in-place physics check, using three perturbation draws per grasp for 30 rollouts. Selection and evaluation used distinct random seeds. Object starts varied within a 5 mm radius and ±10° yaw, and friction coefficients ranged from 0.5 to 0.9. Soup-can and mustard-bottle masses ranged from 0.8 to 1.2 times nominal; cylinder, cube, and box masses remained fixed.

Object Waypoints and Contact-Loss Monitoring

The single-object waypoint trials used Franka-Ability with a soup can and a 20 cm carry. Each stored plan executed once, with friction coefficient 0.7 and acceleration 2.0 m/s². The dual Franka-Ability cohort used serial per-object motions in a persistent scene at these friction and acceleration settings, with one rollout per plan. In separate contact-loss tests, we injected a hand opening during the Franka-Ability carry; control trials omitted this injected opening. The validator monitored contact loss during carry and stopped the task after detected loss.

Simultaneous Dual-Object Manipulation

Simultaneous planning combines the left and right joint trajectories on a common clock. Each shared phase takes the longer of the per-arm durations, synchronizing the manipulation stages. The force-aware trajectory model includes both objects' states and their contact forces.

The A7-L6 bimanual robot manipulator carried a cylinder in its left hand and a soup can in its right. We paired one reused left-hand cylinder grasp with nine right-hand soup-can grasps, giving nine fixed grasp pairs. Each pair received three perturbation draws per seed across three seeds. Both object starts varied within 5 mm and ±10° yaw, and friction ranged from 0.5 to 0.9. Soup-can mass ranged from 0.8 to 1.2 times nominal; cylinder mass remained fixed. The force plan used friction 0.5 and soup-can mass 1.2 times nominal, with exact initial-pose retargeting and fixed-window execution.

Hand-Link Origin Separation

In a separate diagnostic, the requested distance between selected hand-link origins was 20 cm. The observed distance at the final trajectory configuration was 19.76 cm.
